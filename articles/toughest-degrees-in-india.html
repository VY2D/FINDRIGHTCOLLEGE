Toughest Degrees in India — Top 5 Hardest Degrees Explained

The toughest degrees in India are usually considered to be MBBS, BTech in core engineering, Chartered Accountancy (a professional course, not a degree), Bachelor of Architecture, and LLB — tough for their volume of study, length, and exam pressure. But "toughest" depends on the student: a degree is hard when it demands something you are not naturally good at.

Students search for this list for two reasons: some want to test themselves against the hardest challenge; most want to know what they are getting into before they commit 4–5 years. The list is only useful if you understand why each degree is hard, and whether that kind of hard suits you.

What makes a degree "tough"?

A degree becomes tough for a combination of reasons: the volume of syllabus, the length of the course, the difficulty of the exams, the practical training it demands, and the pressure of the profession afterwards. A degree can be tough in one way and gentle in another — medicine on memory, engineering on maths and logic, law on reading and argument.

The five hardest, and why

1. MBBS — Medicine.

MBBS is the longest and most intense undergraduate path: 4.5 years of study plus a 1-year internship, with a syllabus that never seems to end — anatomy, physiology, pathology, pharmacology, then clinical practice with real patients. It is tough on memory, stamina and emotional strength, and it cannot be done online — medicine needs hospitals, labs and patients. Students who love science find it meaningful; students who chose it for family pressure often burn out.

2. BTech — Engineering (core branches).

Engineering is tough on logic and mathematics. Four years of calculus, physics, mechanics, electronics or code, with labs, projects and exams that punish last-minute study. The entrance exam is one of the hardest filters in the country, and the course keeps that pressure going. Like medicine, a real engineering degree needs labs and practical work, so it is not a fully online degree.

3. Chartered Accountancy (CA) — not a degree, but the hardest professional course.

CA is often named in "toughest" lists, so it belongs here with a correction: CA is a professional qualification from ICAI, not a university degree. It is famous for low pass percentages and years of disciplined self-study across accounting, taxation, audit and law. Many students do a B.Com alongside CA, because the degree gives them a fallback and the CA gives them the professional edge. If you love numbers and can study alone for years, CA rewards you.

4. Bachelor of Architecture (B.Arch).

Architecture is 5 years of a different kind of hard: design studios, endless drawings and models, juries where your work is criticised in front of everyone, and a syllabus mixing engineering, art and history. It is tough on creativity under deadline and on the sheer hours the studio demands. Students who love design thrive.

5. LLB — Law.

Law is tough on reading and thinking. Five years (in the integrated BA LLB) of statutes, case law, judgments and legal reasoning, with exams that test argument rather than memory. The best law schools add moot courts, internships and fierce competition. It suits students who love reading, debating and writing.

The degrees people call tough that are actually manageable

Not every respected degree is brutally hard. BBA, BCA, B.Com and BA need consistent study, projects and exams, but they are designed so that an average student who studies regularly can finish them well. That is their strength: a degree you can actually complete, from a UGC-approved university, while building skills, is worth more than a "tough" degree you drop out of in year two.

How to use this list without hurting yourself

  • Do not pick a degree just because it is on a "toughest" list. Prestige fades in the first year; the daily work does not. Pick the difficulty you can live with for 4–5 years.
  • Match the difficulty to your strength. Good at memory and science? Medicine fits. Good at logic and maths? Engineering or CA fits. Good at reading and argument? Law fits. Good with people and business? Management fits.
  • Have a fallback. If you attempt CA, do a B.Com alongside it. If you attempt medicine or engineering, know the exit options. A plan B is not weakness; it is wisdom.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the top 3 hardest degrees?

The three most commonly named are MBBS (medicine), BTech in core engineering, and Chartered Accountancy — though CA is a professional qualification, not a university degree. Medicine is hardest on volume and stamina, engineering on maths and logic, and CA on self-discipline. Which one feels hardest depends on the student's strengths.

Is MBBS the toughest degree in India?

MBBS is usually ranked the toughest because of its length (4.5 years plus a 1-year internship), the volume of the syllabus, and the emotional demands of clinical training. But students who love science often find it more meaningful than difficult, while a student who dislikes memorisation may find engineering harder. Toughness is personal, not absolute.

Is CA tougher than an MBA?

Yes, in terms of exam difficulty and pass rates, CA is widely considered tougher than an MBA. CA demands years of self-study and has very low pass percentages at each level, while an MBA is a structured 2-year university programme with continuous assessment. That is also why many students do a B.Com with CA, or choose an MBA for a faster, more predictable route into management.

Can I do a tough degree online?

Some can and some cannot. Management, commerce, computer applications and arts degrees — BBA, MBA, BCA, MCA, B.Com, BA — are offered as full online degrees by UGC-approved universities. Medicine, engineering, architecture and law need labs, studios, hospitals or court training, so they are not available as fully online degrees.

Ask Your Senior

The toughest degree is not the best degree — the best degree is the toughest one you can finish with your skills intact and your confidence growing. Do not choose suffering as a badge. Choose the hard that matches your mind, keep a fallback ready, and remember that a completed BBA or BCA with real skills will take you further than a half-finished "tough" degree.
